*Fresh Ipsos *'Understanding Society' 2026 data just landed**

**TL;DR:** The UK public is jaded. For tech to land now, it’s less about "novelty" and more about **reliability, integrity, and safety**

3 things for the TBD+ crowd that caught my eye (UK specific):** 🇬🇧📊

**1. AI is Broad but "Thin"** 🤖
We talk about AI adoption constantly, and the reach is there: dedicated AI platforms now hit **46%** of the UK internet pop [1, 2].

But the "stickiness" is lagging. The average user spends about as much time on AI **per month** (2h 13m) as social media users spend on social media **per day** (2h 17m) [2, 3].

We’ve got awareness, but we haven't hit the "daily habit" phase yet. Engagement gap, not a reach gap.

**2. The "Air Fryer" Baseline (Lifestyle Creep is Real)** 🍟
Despite being objectively wealthier (GDP per capita) than in the 80s,
satisfaction with our standard of living has cratered from **72% in 1988 to 52% now** [4, 5].

Why? Our "essential" baseline has shifted. Items that didn't exist or were luxuries are now "can't live without" status:
* **31%** say they can’t live without an air fryer** [6].
* **24%** view a *fortnightly holiday abroad** as a non-negotiable necessity (up from 11% in '89) [6].

**Result: A state of permanent **loss-aversion**. We have more, but we’re more anxious about losing the "new essentials" [6].

**3. The End of Techno-Optimism?** 📉
Have the 80's "move fast and break things" attitudes shifted to "regulate and protect"?

In 1985, **76%** of Brits wanted the gov to do more to encourage new tech; that’s fallen to **49%** [7].
The biggest driver is child safety:
* **86%** are now worried about children’s **social media dependency** [8].
* **72%** of the public actually **support laws** for age-verification to ban under-16s from social platforms [9]. 
* For AI, **67%** see it as a threat to jobs, compared to only 40% who felt that way about IT/Computers in 1997 [3].
