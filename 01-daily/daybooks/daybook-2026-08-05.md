# Daybook — 05 August 2026



Nordics call

## Executive summary

The meeting focused on whether the Nordic organisation should expand use of the **Tools** platform and how it could support the wider Polaris and Business Central transformation.

There was broad alignment around:

- Using Tools as a Nordic platform rather than a country-specific solution.
- Reducing technician dependence on Business Central.
- Taking a phased approach, starting with existing Nordic markets and Denmark discovery.
- Introducing stronger Nordic governance and prioritisation.
- Building a formal business case based on licence savings, process simplification and harmonisation.

The discussion evolved from solution capability into architecture, operating model and programme planning.

## Main themes

### 1. Harmonise the Nordics

The strongest message from both Belron and the Tools team was the desire to create a more consistent Nordic operating model.

Current challenges include:

- Countries raising requirements independently.
- Different priorities between markets.
- Process variation growing over time.

The proposed direction is:

- Common Nordic capabilities.
- Shared governance.
- Controlled localisation only where genuinely needed.
- Reuse of functionality across markets.

**Source: meeting transcript**

### 2. Move technicians out of Business Central

A recurring theme was reducing reliance on BC for technician-facing processes.

The objective is not simply to replace BC with Tools.

Instead, the target architecture discussed is:

- Users work in role-specific applications.
- BC remains an important system of record.
- Integration happens through APIs and an integration platform.
- Users only access systems relevant to their jobs.

Mathias explicitly described a more domain-driven future architecture.

**Source: meeting transcript**

### 3. Start with what already exists

The group favoured an evolutionary approach rather than a large redesign.

Suggestions included:

- Leveraging existing functionality in Norway, Sweden and Finland.
- Using Sweden as an early reference point because it is already on a SaaS BC platform.
- Extending proven capabilities before building new ones.

Robin repeatedly advised keeping scope manageable and avoiding large multi-country design exercises too early.

**Source: meeting transcript**

### 4. Denmark requires dedicated discovery

Denmark was identified as the biggest unknown.

Specific differences discussed included:

- Mobile technician operations.
- Mobile static recalibration capability.
- A customised technician application.
- Different operational processes.

Robin proposed visiting Denmark and spending time with technicians to understand real-world workflows before finalising plans.

This proposal received strong support.

**Source: meeting transcript**

### 5. Governance needs to improve

The current model is largely country-led.

Mikkel outlined a future model based on:

- Nordic governance.
- Shared prioritisation.
- Better visibility of change requests.
- Reduced noise from competing local demands.

Jira and Azure DevOps were discussed as possible mechanisms for managing demand.

**Source: meeting transcript**

### 6. Tools is being treated as a formal supplier

Although relationships appear positive, Mikkel was clear that Tools is being assessed alongside other suppliers.

The team requested:

- A delivery approach.
- Workshop plans.
- Resource assumptions.
- Project dependencies.
- Timeline estimates.
- Commercial costs.

The intention is to compare suppliers consistently.

## My architecture assessment

The most important outcome was not a technology decision.

It was alignment around a future operating model:

- Shared Nordic capabilities.
- Product-style governance.
- Domain ownership.
- API-first integration.
- Reduced BC user footprint.
- Incremental modernisation.

That's a sensible direction because it focuses on business capabilities and outcomes rather than replacing one application with another.

The biggest unresolved decision is governance. The long-term success of the platform will depend less on technology choices and more on who owns prioritisation, standards and country-level exceptions. Without that, the platform is likely to drift back towards local customisation over time.
